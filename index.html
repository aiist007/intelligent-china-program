China is no longer just a market to enter or a supply base to manage. In electric vehicles, smart manufacturing, and AI plus robotics, China has become a living laboratory for industrial-scale innovation. This program helps global executives understand how China builds sectoral leadership, where partnership opportunities exist, and what can only be learned by being on the ground.

Program Overview

Intelligent China is a 12-day executive immersion designed for global business leaders who need a practical, on-the-ground understanding of China's next-generation industrial capabilities. Delivered across three four-day modules in Beijing, Shenzhen, and Shanghai & Hangzhou, the program combines CKGSB faculty sessions, industry expert briefings, company visits, closed-door executive dialogues, and investor roundtables.

This is not a conventional China study tour. It is a business-oriented executive learning journey focused on three sectors where China has developed significant scale advantages: electric vehicles, smart manufacturing, and AI plus robotics. The program helps international executives understand how China builds sectoral leadership, and how these capabilities can translate into partnership, investment, sourcing, market-entry, technology adoption, and organizational transformation opportunities.

"Disrupt or be disrupted" cannot be truer than in the China industrial story of the last decade. What remains certain is the speed, depth, and global relevance of the country's industrial transformation. Tailored for senior executives who must convert China immersion into business action, this program equips participants with the field intelligence, executive access, and strategic frameworks required to act — not just to observe.

Program Highlights

Three Cities, Three Industrial Lenses

Spend four days each in Beijing, Shenzhen, and Shanghai & Hangzhou — four city-regions that together account for a disproportionate share of China's EV, smart manufacturing, and AI ecosystems. Each module is built around a single, focused industrial question, and concludes with an on-site partnership workshop.

Field Learning, Not Showroom Tours

Move from morning classroom sessions to afternoon company visits, institutional briefings, and closed-door executive dialogues. Evenings are reserved for alumni dinners, investor salons, and reflection workshops — designed to convert observation into strategic judgment.

Closed-Door Access to Decision Makers

Engage directly with company executives, founders, and industry experts — including CKGSB's alumni network, which leads one-fifth of China's most valuable brands. The program prioritizes private, off-the-record exchanges over standard corporate hospitality.

China vs Global Benchmarking in Every Module

Each module includes structured comparisons with global peers (e.g. Xiaomi Auto / Li Auto / NIO vs Tesla / Toyota / VW; Shenzhen hardware ecosystem vs Siemens / Bosch / Apple supply chain; Alibaba Cloud / Tongyi / Unitree vs OpenAI / Anthropic / NVIDIA ecosystem) to avoid a China-only narrative.

A Capstone You Can Use at Home

Every participant or company team develops a China Industrial Opportunity Memo across the program, updated after each module and presented in the final workshop. The memo answers four questions: What can we learn from China? Where can we partner? What should we invest in or monitor? What risks or misconceptions should we avoid?

Learning Approach

A Four-Part Learning Loop in Every Module

  1. Context — CKGSB faculty and industry experts frame the macro, policy, and industry structure.
  2. Case — Classroom discussion of representative Chinese and global companies and business models.
  3. Field — Afternoon visits to factories, R&D centers, platforms, regulators, and research institutions.
  4. Action — Evening reflection or Day 4 workshop translating observations into partnership, sourcing, and investment opportunities.

Industrial Capital Evenings

Each module may include an investor salon with USD funds, RMB funds, corporate venture capital, industry investors, and family offices. Discussion topics may include: which companies represent real industrial trends, which valuations or business models may be overhyped, which cooperation opportunities are more practical than direct investment, and which sectors are creating cross-border M&A, technology licensing, or supply-chain restructuring opportunities.

Module 3 · Shanghai & Hangzhou — AI, Robotics, and Platform Intelligence

Module positioning. Module 3 is delivered across two complementary cities. Shanghai anchors the module with a curated set of flagship AI visits: SenseTime (multimodal AI platform), Stepfun (foundation model), AgiBot (humanoid robotics), and Biren Technology (AI compute). The two cities, connected by a one-hour high-speed rail, jointly define the Chinese AI / robotics landscape — and Module 3 is structured as a single four-day arc that moves between them.

Capstone: The China Industrial Opportunity Memo

Each participant or company team develops an action memo throughout the program. The memo is updated after each module and presented at the end of the Shanghai & Hangzhou module.

Suggested structure

  1. My industry's China exposure and opportunity
  2. What I learned from China's AI, EV, and smart manufacturing ecosystems
  3. Potential partners and collaboration models (OEM, ODM, JVs, licensing, sourcing, investment)
  4. Investment or market-entry themes to monitor
  5. Risks, constraints, and misconceptions (policy, compliance, data, export control, IP, geopolitics, cultural)
  6. 90-day action plan after returning home
